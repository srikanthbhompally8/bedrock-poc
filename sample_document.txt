Project Aurora — Internal Briefing

Project Aurora is a company-wide initiative to modernize the image-delivery pipeline.
It launched on March 3, 2025 and is led by the Platform Infrastructure team. The goal
is to cut the median image-processing latency from 820 milliseconds to under 300
milliseconds by the end of Q4 2025.

The rollout has three phases. Phase 1 (April–June) migrates the storage layer to a
regional object store. Phase 2 (July–September) introduces an edge caching tier in
five regions: us-east-1, us-west-2, eu-west-1, ap-south-1, and sa-east-1. Phase 3
(October–December) rewrites the transform service in Rust and enables autoscaling.

Budget for the fiscal year is 2.4 million dollars, with roughly 60 percent allocated
to the edge caching tier. The primary risk called out by the steering committee is
regional capacity during the Phase 2 edge rollout, particularly in ap-south-1, where
GPU instances are frequently constrained.

The executive sponsor is Dana Whitfield, VP of Infrastructure. Weekly status updates
are published every Friday to the #aurora-status channel.
